# The Purple Teaming Concept: How Red and Blue Team Collaboration Is Changing Defense Strategy in Modern Organizations

We always hear about the Blue Team for defense and the Red Team for offense, and most of us want to break into one of these two teams... but did you know there's a third team? It's a blend of both, focused on protection while simultaneously building out defense mechanisms and reporting, and it's actually close to the **GRC** field (Governance, Risk, Compliance).

## What Is the Purple Team?

The Purple Team isn't a separate, standalone third team next to Red and Blue. It's a way of getting the two teams to work together instead of each one operating in its own lane.

Picture it this way: the Red Team attacks systems to find weaknesses, like a professional thief you hire to try breaking into your house and show you where the weak doors are. The Blue Team is the one monitoring and defending, like home security watching the cameras and reacting if a break-in happens.

The problem with the old approach: the Red Team attacks, writes a massive report full of vulnerabilities, and hands it off to the Blue Team... and that's it. The Blue Team reads the report weeks later, without really knowing how the attack unfolded step by step.

Purple Teaming solves this: both teams work at the same time. Red attacks using a specific tactic, while Blue monitors in real time through tools like SIEM (a system that collects and alerts on security events) and EDR (a tool that watches endpoints and detects suspicious behavior). If the attack goes undetected, they adjust the monitoring rules right away, instead of waiting for a final report.

## Why Does This Matter?

- **Cuts down detection time:** instead of discovering an attack hours or days later, detection becomes far faster because the adjustment happens during the exercise itself.
- **Saves the organization money:** it helps uncover misconfigured security tools, or expensive tools nobody is actually using.
- **Keeps up with new threats:** with AI entering everything, there's growing focus on protecting machine learning models themselves from attacks like data poisoning.

## A Simplified Comparison Table

| | Red Team | Blue Team | Purple Team |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Role** | Attacks and simulates intruders | Monitors and defends | Coordinates between both teams |
| **Focus** | Uncovering vulnerabilities | Maintaining business continuity | Turning attack results into immediate defensive action |
| **Goal** | Testing system resilience | Reducing detection and response time | Raising overall cyber resilience |

## Frameworks Used in This Field

If you're studying this area, you'll run into these names a lot:

- **MITRE ATT&CK:** a global reference that classifies real attacker tactics, used by both Red and Blue teams as a shared language.
- **PTEF (Purple Team Exercise Framework):** a framework that defines how Purple Team exercises are designed and run from start to finish.
